# Founder Decision Gates

These decisions intentionally belong to Sarab. Agents should bring evidence and recommendations, not silently choose. This is how Singh Dynamics stays a thinking business rather than an AI-following exercise.

## Gate 1: Initial customer profile

Current hypothesis: US B2B service companies using QuickBooks Online + Google Workspace.

Before commercial pilot, review evidence from at least 10 target businesses and answer:
1. Which type of business has enough overdue invoice pain to pay for this?
2. Who feels the pain: owner, bookkeeper, AR clerk, operations manager?
3. Roughly how many invoices per month make Singh AR worth connecting?
4. What do they currently do when an invoice is late?
5. What would make them refuse to let software email customers?

Founder output: a one-paragraph ICP and reasons.

## Gate 2: Pilot authority

Choose what the first design partner lets Singh do:
- draft only
- send routine reminders with approval
- send routine reminders automatically

Default recommendation is approval-only first. Founder must explicitly expand authority.

## Gate 3: Tone

Review at least three examples for friendly, firm, and final routine reminders. Define what feels professional and what crosses the line.

Founder output: approved tone examples, not just adjectives.

## Gate 4: Payment-plan authority

Do not automate this based on imagination. After at least ten real payment-plan decisions from a design partner, review the decisions and decide whether a deterministic bounded rule is justified.

Questions:
- minimum upfront percentage?
- maximum extension?
- maximum invoice size?
- customer history requirements?
- any categories that always require approval?

## Gate 5: Pricing

Do not let an agent choose the final price. Agents can research competitors and model value. Founder chooses the experiment.

Candidate value metrics to evaluate later:
- invoices monitored
- monthly AR volume
- active overdue cases
- flat subscription tiers

Avoid percentage-of-debt-recovered pricing until legal/business implications are reviewed.

## Gate 6: Autonomy expansion

Every new action class requires evidence:
- number of times humans made the decision
- agreement rate between Singh recommendation and human choice
- cost of a wrong decision
- reversibility

Founder decides whether to move an action from approval to autonomous.

## Gate 7: Pivot/kill decision

After a meaningful pilot period, answer:
- Is AR pain real enough?
- Will users connect accounting + email?
- Does automation actually reduce work?
- Is willingness to pay credible?
- Do you still want to build in this problem space?

If the answer is no, preserve the platform and change the workflow. Do not keep the product alive just because code exists.
